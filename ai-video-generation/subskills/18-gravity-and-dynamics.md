# Subskill 18 — Gravity and Dynamics

## Purpose
Keep weight, momentum, balance, falling, bouncing, and fluid motion believable.

## Gravity and support
- Everything not supported falls toward the ground. Nothing hovers without a stated cause.
- Support must be visible: ground, table, hand, hook, string.
- If a held object is released, it should fall or rest on a surface. "Hover after release" is always a defect unless designed.

## Weight and effort cues
Weight is communicated through the *person* as much as the object:
- Heavy: slower start, arm tension, torso lean toward the opposite side, slightly bent knees, stronger grip.
- Light: quick lift, relaxed posture, small wobble.
- Liquid-filled container: heavier; contents slosh with motion; surface tilts level against gravity.
Prompt tip: name the effort ("she leans slightly to counterbalance the full can") instead of only the weight.

## Momentum and inertia
- Starting takes effort; stopping takes distance. Objects overshoot slightly when stopped abruptly.
- Swinging objects (a hanging handle, a bucket) lag behind the hand, then swing and settle.
- Turning while carrying shifts the load outward.
- Avoid instant direction changes of heavy or fast objects.

## Balance
- The center of mass stays over the base of support. Carrying on one side requires lean/counterweight.
- Single-foot stances, tiptoe reaching, and deep squats are higher risk (R2+).
- Reach has limits: do not stretch arms beyond body proportion to reach distant objects; move the feet instead.

## Falling and bouncing
- Falling objects accelerate (they do not drift at constant speed) and land with a response.
- Bounce height decreases each bounce (energy loss). Soft objects barely bounce.
- Small objects look "fast" when they fall short distances; avoid slow-motion cues that imply the wrong scale unless intended.
- Landing response depends on material: dust, splash, deformation, sound.

## Friction and sliding
- Objects on surfaces decelerate and stop; they do not glide forever.
- Pushing a heavy object needs visible effort; light objects move easily.
- Feet should not slide relative to the ground while the body travels.

## Rotation and torque
- Rotation originates from a force applied off-axis. Door hinges, knobs, and levers rotate about fixed axes.
- A tilted container pivots about its supported edge or the hand's grip.

## Fluids (pouring, spraying, splashing)
Fluids are R3–R4 when the level or target must change. Checklist:
- Liquid exits the spout/lip only after the container tilts past the pouring threshold.
- The stream follows a gravity arc and lands on the target.
- Source level falls; target level rises or the surface darkens/wets.
- Liquid does not flow upward, hover, or appear from nowhere.
- Droplet spray (a watering-can rose) is often easier than a thin continuous stream; consider it when exact stream behavior is not required.
- After pouring, wet marks, puddles, or darker soil should persist (state continuity).

## Cloth, hair, and secondary motion
- Cloth and hair react to body motion and wind with a slight lag. Wind direction must be consistent across all elements.

## Reliable zone vs risky zone (reported by tests; verify on your model)
- Often reliable: ambient motion, light, weather, simple cloth and fluid motion that does not need to change a state.
- Often risky: breaking, shattering, precise liquid state change, chained collisions, multiple interacting objects, and long continuous takes.
Use reliable motion to carry the shot's richness and keep risky contact short and simple.

## Prompting dynamics
- State material and weight: "a full metal watering can".
- State trajectory and endpoints: "the water arcs from the spout down onto the tomato plants".
- Keep speed consistent: avoid "slow motion" and "fast" in the same sentence.
- Do not mix incompatible physics unless intended.

## Checklist
- [ ] Nothing hovers; support is visible
- [ ] Weight cues in the body and object
- [ ] Starting/stopping with believable inertia
- [ ] Balance and counterweight when carrying
- [ ] Falling accelerates; bounce loses energy
- [ ] Fluids follow gravity and change state
- [ ] Wind/secondary motion directions agree
