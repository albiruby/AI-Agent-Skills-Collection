# Subskill 05 — Camera and Shot Design

## Purpose
Design a shot that communicates the action and suits the visual style.

## Choose framing by purpose
- Wide/establishing: geography, group blocking, environment.
- Medium-wide: full action and object interaction.
- Medium: gestures and upper-body interaction.
- Close-up: expression, detail, product feature.
- Insert: critical object or hand action.

## Camera specification
Specify only useful controls:
- Shot size and angle.
- Camera height and orientation.
- Static, pan, tilt, dolly, tracking, handheld, or orbit.
- Subject position and screen direction.
- Focus/depth behavior if supported.
- Whether the shot is continuous or edited.

## Composition
- Keep important subjects and props visible.
- Avoid unnecessary occlusion.
- Provide enough screen space for movement.
- Maintain readable separation in group scenes.
- Preserve screen direction across cuts.
- Avoid excessive camera motion that competes with the action.

## Single shot vs multi-shot
Use one continuous shot for simple everyday actions, demonstrations, or scenes where spatial clarity matters. Use multiple shots when the idea requires a change in viewpoint, detail, or location. Do not add cuts merely to make a video feel cinematic.

## Output
Describe framing, angle, movement, subject placement, and cut strategy in plain language.
