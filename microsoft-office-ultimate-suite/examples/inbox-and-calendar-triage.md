# Worked Example: Inbox and Calendar Triage

## Scenario

A manager asks: "My inbox is out of control. Clean it up, flag what needs a reply today, and prepare next week's meetings. Don't send anything without asking."

## 1. Intake and authorization

- Mailbox: manager's own mailbox; shared team mailbox is out of scope unless approved.
- Allowed: read, categorize, move, draft replies, propose meeting times.
- Not allowed: send, delete permanently, change mailbox permissions, invite attendees without confirmation.
- Time zone and working hours: confirm before scheduling.

## 2. Inspect

- Identify high-volume senders, distribution lists, automated notifications, and real threads.
- Check existing folders, categories, rules, and flags to avoid duplicate structures.
- Identify messages requiring a decision, a reply, a delegation, or no action.
- Check calendar for conflicts, focus time, recurring meetings, and travel.

## 3. Triage design

Suggested categories:

- `Action today`
- `Action this week`
- `Waiting on others`
- `FYI / read later`
- `Archive candidate`

Rules:

- Do not auto-delete; move to archive only with a clear, reversible rule.
- Do not auto-forward outside the organization.
- Keep VIP/legal/HR messages out of automated rules.
- Create rules only after confirming the pattern with the user.

## 4. Draft replies

- Draft only; do not send.
- Verify recipients, thread context, commitments, attachments, and tone.
- Mark placeholders where facts are missing.
- Summarize long threads and state the requested action.

## 5. Calendar

- Propose times rather than booking when attendees are external or senior.
- Check time zones, recurrence, room/resource needs, and conflicts.
- Add agenda, duration, and preparation notes.
- Do not invite attendees without explicit confirmation.

## 6. Record in OneNote

Create a page with:

- Today's priorities and why.
- Waiting-on list with owners/dates.
- Decisions needed.
- Meeting prep links.

## 7. Validation and handoff

- Confirm counts: messages categorized, drafts created, rules proposed/created, meetings proposed.
- Report what was not touched (shared mailbox, external invites, deletions).
- Provide a rollback note for any rule or move.
- Ask for confirmation before sending or inviting.

## 8. What commonly goes wrong

- Sending instead of drafting.
- Reply All when only the sender needed a response.
- Deleting instead of archiving.
- Creating rules that move security/HR mail out of sight.
- Booking meetings without checking time zones or conflicts.
- Reporting "inbox zero" when messages were merely hidden.
